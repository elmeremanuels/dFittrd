# Measuring a garment for Curtain: 5 numbers per size

For shop owners. Time: about 3 minutes per size. You need a flat table, a tape measure and the garment. Write centimetres.

Why we ask: your size chart tells us which **body** a size is made for. It does not tell us how big the **garment** is: how far the waistband stretches, how wide the hem swings. With these numbers we can tell a shopper "the band stretches over your hips with 3 cm to spare" instead of guessing, and we can stop recommending a size that she could not pull on.

## In short

1. **Five numbers per size**: waistband relaxed (A), waistband stretched (B), hip at 20 cm below the band (C), hem (D), length (E).
2. **On at least three sizes: the smallest, the largest and one in between.** Every size is better.
3. **Once per garment: how it closes.** Pull-on, zip, buttons or drawstring. For a pull-on garment this is the answer that matters most.
4. **Optional: the weight** of one size on a kitchen scale. Skip it if it is any trouble.

## How to lay the garment

1. Lay it flat on a table, front side up, closed (zip or buttons done up), not on a hanger and not on a mannequin.
2. Smooth it with your hands. Do not pull it, except in step B.
3. Front and back edges on top of each other, side seams at the outer edges.

Every width is measured **straight across, edge to edge**, and then **multiplied by 2**. Write down both: the flat width and the doubled number. (If the two do not match, we see it and ask.)

## The 5 measurements (skirt or trousers with a waistband)

| | What | How |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Waistband, relaxed | Across the top edge of the waistband, lying as it falls, not pulled. x 2. |
| **B** | Waistband, stretched | Same place. Pull the band as far as you would pull it to step into the garment. Do not force it. x 2. If the band does not stretch, write the same number as A. If it clearly goes further without strain, write that number next to it too. |
| **C** | Hip, at 20 cm | Lay the tape **20 cm below the top edge of the waistband**, parallel to the band, and measure straight across. x 2. Always 20 cm, for every size and every garment, so the sizes can be compared. Put a pin at 20 cm on each side first if that helps. **If you measure at another distance, write the distance next to the number**: a hip number without its distance cannot be used. |
| **D** | Hem | Across the bottom edge, with the hem spread flat. x 2. On a wide skirt the hem will not lie in a straight line: follow the hem edge with the tape from one side fold to the other. |
| **E** | Length | From the top edge of the waistband straight down to the hem, at the centre front. Not doubled. |

The 20 cm for C is our choice, not an industry rule: it is roughly where the hips are widest below the waist on an adult, and one fixed distance is what makes sizes comparable. On a skirt that gets wider towards the hem, measuring 3 cm lower gives 4 to 5 cm more, so the distance matters as much as the number. If the gathered or elastic part of the band itself is deeper than about 10 cm, tell us how deep it is.

## Which sizes

- **Best: every size.** It is 3 minutes each.
- **Minimum: three sizes: the smallest, the largest and one in between.** We estimate the sizes between them and label them "estimated" for the shopper.
- **The smallest and the largest matter most.** We can only estimate well *between* measured sizes. A size beyond the measured ones is a "rough estimate": nothing holds the number in place, we tell the shopper so, and we are less sure of our advice for it.
- Never fill in a size you did not measure. Leave it empty. We keep track of which numbers are yours and which are our estimates, and we never show an estimate as a measurement.

## Once per garment

**Required:**

- **How does it close?** One of: **pull-on** (elastic band, nothing to open), **zip**, **buttons**, **drawstring** (the waist opens wider when the cord is loosened), **wrap**, or something else (say what). For a pull-on garment the stretched band (B) has to go over the hips, so we check B against the shopper's hips and do not recommend a size whose band is too small. For a garment that opens we do not make that check.

**Helpful, if you know it:**

- **Where is it meant to sit?** At the waist, just below the navel, or on the hip.
- **How high is the waistband** (top edge to the seam under it)?
- **Is the skirt gathered into the band?** Is there a slit (which side, how long from the hem up), are there tiers, is it lined?

**Optional:**

- **Weight.** Weigh one size on a kitchen scale and tell us which size. It helps the picture of how the fabric hangs; it does not change the size advice. Leave it out if it is any trouble.

For tops and dresses the idea is the same, with other places: chest straight across under the armholes (x 2), waist at the narrowest point or the waist seam (x 2), hem (x 2), and length from the highest point of the shoulder to the hem. Say where you measured.

## Worked example: Crimson Wildflower Maxi Skirt

These are the shop's numbers (received 13 November 2026), laid out in this form. Column C was measured at a distance below the band that the shop did not write down, so it is **not** yet a 20 cm measurement:

| Size | A band relaxed | B band stretched | C hip (distance not stated) | D hem | E length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AU 8 | 33.5 flat → **67** | 47 flat → **94** | 56 flat → **112** | 115 flat → **230** | **103** |
| AU 10 | 35.5 flat → **71** | 50 flat → **100** | 61 flat → **122** | 124 flat → **248** | **103** |
| AU 12 | not measured | | | | |
| AU 14 | 40 flat → **80** | 55 flat → **110** | 63 flat → **126** | 130 flat → **260** | **104** |
| AU 16 | not measured | | | | |

(The bold doubled numbers are the shop's. The flat widths are those numbers divided by 2, worked back by us to show the form, except the AU 8 and AU 10 hem, which the shop wrote flat.)

Closure: **pull-on**, elastic band, no zip and no drawstring (told to us on 15 November 2026; still to be confirmed with the shop).

What we do with it:

- The band, pulled as far as the shop found comfortable, is 94 cm in AU 8, and the size chart says AU 8 is for 94 cm hips (AU 10: 100 against 99; AU 14: 110 against 109). So each measured size goes over the hips it is made for with 0 to 1 cm to spare, the size below it is 4 to 6 cm too small, and the size above has 5 to 6 cm to spare. We could not have seen that from the size chart.
- Because the skirt is pull-on, a shopper with 100 cm hips and a 73.5 cm waist is never offered AU 8 (the band is 6 cm short), even though the band would hold at her waist. She is offered AU 10 with the remark that the band only just goes over her hips. With 104 cm hips and a 77.5 cm waist she is offered AU 12, and AU 10 is ruled out (4 cm short).
- "Too small" here means more than 2.5 cm short at the pull the shop called comfortable. A band can be pulled further than is comfortable, so this is our caution, not a hard limit. If your B was a gentle pull and the band goes clearly further, tell us that number: it changes the advice.
- The hem is 2.3 to 2.6 metres around: a real, wide A-line. Our earlier guess was 2 metres.
- AU 12 and AU 16 were not measured. AU 12 lies between two measured sizes, so we estimate it (band 75.5 relaxed, 105 stretched) and label it "estimated". AU 16 lies beyond the largest measured size, so its numbers (84.5 and 115) are a "rough estimate" and our advice for that size is less sure: please measure AU 16.

What we still need to ask for this skirt:

1. **At what distance below the band was the hip (C) measured?** The hip numbers jump unevenly (112, 122, 126). Please measure C again at exactly 20 cm below the top of the band, on all sizes.
2. **AU 16 and AU 12**, all five numbers. AU 16 first.
3. **Confirm the closure**: pull-on, no zip, no drawstring.
4. **Does the band go clearly further than B without strain?** If so, how far.
5. Helpful: where the skirt is meant to sit, how high the band is, whether there is a slit or a lining. Optional: the weight of one size.
