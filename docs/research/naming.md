# Naming

**Chosen name: Paskamer.**

Method: generated 12 candidates (Fittrd/dFittrd, DressingRoom, Paskamer, Mirra, Dapper, Snug, Fitsy, Drape, Tryon, Hangr, Fitly, SuitsMe), ran web searches per name in the fashion / virtual try-on / Shopify-plugin context, then a second round for replacements (Mooi, Looksie, Fitje, Doppel, Pashok, FitCheck) and deeper checks on survivors. Hard conflicts found and discarded: Mirra (MIRRARME, Try with Mirra, Mirrafit, MirraLook), Dapper (Dapper Shopping Inc.), Drape (Drape LLC consumer app + 'Drape AI' Shopify try-on apps), Tryon (dozens of try-on apps, generic), Hangr (HangRr, Hangr.fr, The Hangr), Fitly (Fitly.ai, Fittly Shopify, two Fitly iOS apps), SuitsMe (SUITSME dress-up game), Snug (Snug Size Charts Shopify app plus many Class 25 marks), Fitsy (fitness app, dead Swiss mark; 'Fit-' cluster), Looksie (Looksie Style AI fitting room), Doppel (Google Doppl), FitCheck (5+ Shopify try-on apps). dFittrd itself has no exact hit but is unpronounceable/unspellable, the 'd' prefix reads as a typo, and it sits next to Fittr (large Indian fitness platform), Fittd, Fittly and Fits; refined 'Fittrd' inherits the Fittr problem. DressingRoom is generic and crowded (Gap, Samsung Dressroom, Looklet, PerfectCorp, 'Virtual Dressing Room' app).

Why Paskamer: it is the only candidate that scores on every founder criterion at once. (1) Fun and low-key: it is the ordinary Dutch word for the fitting room, not a tech-y coinage. (2) 'Real shopping experience': the fitting room is the most human moment of shopping, and 'shop together' (friends outside the curtain) and the privacy story ('what happens in the paskamer stays in the paskamer' — raw photos never leave the device) both hang naturally off it. (3) Two-sided: 'Try in Paskamer' is a clean merchant button; 'Paskamer for Shopify / WooCommerce' is a clean plugin name. (4) Dutch and English: Dutch users get it instantly; English users get an easy, slightly exotic brand word with a one-line tagline ('your private fitting room'). (5) International: outside the Benelux it is an arbitrary mark, which is a stronger trademark position than at home, and no fashion-tech use was found anywhere. Compared with the runner-up Fitje, it avoids the saturated 'Fit-' cluster and the direct 'Fits' competitor; compared with Mooi, it avoids Moooi B.V.'s active oppositions; compared with Pashok, it carries meaning in the launch market rather than being a blank word.

Caveats, stated plainly: this is not legal advice and web search is not a trademark search. 'Paskamer' is descriptive in NL/BE, so a plain word mark may be refused there and protection may rest on a stylised mark plus the composite 'Paskamer' + logo; an Amsterdam restaurant uses the name (different class). Domain availability was not verified; paskamer.app and paskamer.nl are the first to check. Recommended next steps: check BOIP/EUIPO/USPTO registers for 'Paskamer' in classes 9, 35, 42 (and 25 for adjacency), check app-store name availability on iOS/Android and the Shopify App Store, secure domains and social handles, then have a trademark attorney run a clearance search before the public launch. If the founder wants a name that is phonetically obvious to English speakers from day one, Fitje is the fallback. In code, the rename is mechanical: 4 occurrences of 'DressingRoom' in /home/user/dfittrd/src and the SDK/CDN names in /home/user/dfittrd/docs/HANDOFF.md section 3.2.

## Shortlist

### Paskamer

Dutch for 'fitting room' — it is literally the product, in the founder's own words ('kledingpassen app', 'changingroom'). Low-key and human: the paskamer is the most honest, intimate moment of real shopping, which is exactly the experience the founder wants. Abroad it reads as an arbitrary, pronounceable brand word (pas-KAH-mer, like Zalando or Vinted), so it is MORE distinctive internationally than at home. Works as a verb-ish phrase in NL ('even in de Paskamer') and as a clean button label in EN ('Try in Paskamer'). No app, startup or fashion-tech company called Paskamer was found in any search.

- **Risks:** In the Benelux the word is descriptive for exactly this service, so a bare word mark could be refused or be weak there; you would likely need a stylised/logo mark or a composite. English speakers cannot spell it from hearing it the first time ('pass-kamer'? 'pas-camer'?), and 'kamer' means nothing to them without the tagline. An Amsterdam tapas restaurant named Paskamer exists (different class, low risk). The 'fitting room' metaphor somewhat under-sells the social 'shop together' and Style-DNA parts.
- **Domain ideas:** paskamer.app, paskamer.nl, paskamer.io, getpaskamer.com, paskamer.shop
- **Trademark notes:** No conflicting fashion/retail/try-on use found via web search. Descriptiveness is the main issue in NL/BE (BOIP absolute grounds), much less so for EUIPO/USPTO where it is a foreign non-English word, though EUIPO also examines Dutch meaning. Not legal advice; a professional Benelux + EU clearance search (classes 9, 35, 42) is needed before committing.

### Fitje

Dutch Gen-Z slang for an outfit ('fit' + the -je diminutive): fun, warm, and speaks the target user's own language. 'Fit' carries double meaning in English (an outfit; does it fit?), which is precisely the product's promise. Short, lowercase-friendly, easy to brand (coral 'fitje' wordmark). Merchant copy writes itself: 'Try the fitje', 'Pas je fitje'. No app or company named Fitje was found.

- **Risks:** The 'Fit-' cluster is extremely crowded in this exact category (Fits/fits-app.com, Fittly, Fitted, FitX, FitCheck, Fitsbest, Fittr) so confusion and SEO dilution are real, and 'Fits' is a direct competitor with a near-identical sound. English speakers will say 'fit-jee' instead of 'fit-yuh', so the brand needs to accept both. Slang can age quickly. FITJEANS (denim brand) is a loose neighbour.
- **Domain ideas:** fitje.app, fitje.nl, fitje.io, fitje.shop, getfitje.com
- **Trademark notes:** No exact mark found in search, but 'fit'-based marks are numerous in classes 9/25/42 (e.g. FITLY, FITZY, FITAPP registrations in the US). Expect likelihood-of-confusion objections from one of them. Not legal advice; clearance search required.

### Pashok

From 'pashokje', the Dutch colloquial word for the little fitting cubicle. Cheeky, low-key, very 'real shopping'. Internationally it is a punchy two-syllable invented word (pa-SHOK) with no existing fashion-tech meaning, so it is ownable almost everywhere. Zero conflicts found for 'Pashok' as a brand, company or app.

- **Risks:** The joke only lands in NL/BE; everywhere else it is a blank word you must fill with meaning (budget for that). Contains 'shock', which some may read negatively. In Russian 'Pashok' is a nickname for Pavel (harmless, but it exists). Phonetic neighbour 'Pashtush' (Indian shawl brand with an app) exists but is a different word and category.
- **Domain ideas:** pashok.app, pashok.nl, pashok.com, pashok.io
- **Trademark notes:** Nothing relevant found via web search in fashion, retail or software. Appears to be the cleanest of the five from a conflict standpoint, but the lack of conflicts is also because it is an obscure word. Not legal advice; clearance search required.

### Mooi

Dutch for 'beautiful / nice' and the exact word a friend says in the fitting room ('mooi!'). One syllable, universally pronounceable ('moy'), warm and low-key, and it doubles as the app's reaction/emoji for the shop-together feature. Works as a brand in both languages without translation.

- **Risks:** Moooi B.V. (the famous Breda design brand) holds MOOOI registrations in multiple classes including Class 25 (clothing) and has a track record of opposing similar marks at BOIP ('ZOMOOI', 'Mooy'). Opposition risk is therefore high in the Benelux and plausibly the EU. 'Mooi' is also a very common generic word used by countless Dutch salons, shops and products; mooi.com is taken (Moooi added the extra 'o' for that reason). Strong name, weak ownership.
- **Domain ideas:** mooi.app, getmooi.com, mooi.io, mooi.shop
- **Trademark notes:** Active, litigious prior mark (MOOOI, Moooi B.V.) in adjacent and overlapping classes; a dead US 'MOOI' mark (Mooi Style, Inc.) also exists. I would not proceed without a formal opinion. Not legal advice.

### DressingRoom

The current prototype name. Instantly understood in English (and by Dutch users), describes the product with zero explanation, and is already wired into the code, SDK naming (cdn.dressingroom.app, data-dressingroom-product) and the handoff document. Lowest switching cost.

- **Risks:** Generic and descriptive: effectively unregistrable as a word mark and undefendable. Heavily used in the exact category — Gap's 'DressingRoom by Gap' pilot (2017), 'Virtual Dressing Room' iOS app, Samsung 'Dressroom', Looklet 'Dressing Room', PerfectCorp's 'Virtual Dressing Room' API, plus 'virtual dressing room' as the industry's generic term. SEO is a lost cause, app-store search will be flooded, and dressingroom.com/.app are almost certainly taken. Best kept as a descriptor/tagline, not the brand.
- **Domain ideas:** dressingroom.app (likely taken), mydressingroom.app, dressingroom.nl (likely taken)
- **Trademark notes:** 'Dressing room' / 'virtual dressing room' is generic for the service; expect refusal on absolute grounds at BOIP, EUIPO and USPTO for a word mark. Only a highly stylised logo would be registrable, and it would not stop others using the words. Not legal advice.
